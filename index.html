Findings

What the analysis showed

Six lenses on growth quality. Where a metric is project-derived rather than company-reported, it is labelled as such.

Customer depth is improving

1.51 products per member

Products grew +39% against member growth of +35%. SoFi reported that 43% of new products came from existing members. Products per member is a project-derived ratio from reported counts; it is not a separate official KPI.

Growth is profitable

31.3% adj. EBITDA margin

Revenue of $1.10B (+43% YoY) came with GAAP net income of $166.7M, more than double the prior year. This is not growth bought with losses.

Funding context — one ratio, not a verdict

95.4% deposit-to-loan

The project divides approximately $40.24B in deposits by $42.17B in loans. This single ratio does not establish how the loan book is funded or prove overall funding quality; funding mix, deposit concentration, costs and liquidity would also need review.

Credit risk: one watch item

6.68% credit-card NCO

The total net charge-off rate of ~2.04% is manageable, but credit-card charge-offs at ~6.68% are the clearest risk signal in the lending stack and warrant monitoring.

The cash conversion question

-$2.31B operating cash flow

GAAP net income of +$166.7M sits against operating cash flow of -$2.31B — a $2.48B gap. Rather than scoring this good or bad, the analysis asks the more useful question: what is happening to cash conversion as the business scales?

Customer friction (external proxy)

63.92 complaints / M members

The project reports 940 CFPB complaints for Q1 2026 and normalizes this against the member base. This figure is provisional: the source export, checksum and exact denominator are not committed in the repository, so it cannot currently be reproduced from the published files. CFPB complaints are an external, non-representative proxy, not internal SoFi support data.

Segment diversification

Project-derived shares of reportable segment net revenue (sum of the three segments: approximately $1.146B), not consolidated net revenue (approximately $1.100B).

Decision Framework

Growth Quality Index (GQI)

I designed this scorecard to make eight evidence dimensions easier to discuss. It displays analyst-assigned raw signal scores on a consistent 1–5 scale; weights are separate, judgment-led priorities, not a validated model. Hover a row for the evidence and caveats.

The displayed value is the raw signal score from 1 to 5. Analyst weights are judgment-led and not empirically calibrated. Weighted contributions are not raw signal scores, and no aggregate GQI total is reported because its meaning has not been validated. This is an independent portfolio framework, not an official SoFi metric or management scorecard. See methodology/GQI_Methodology.md and documents/CFPB_Evidence_Notes.md.

Executive recommendations

Requirements discipline

12 key business questions decomposed into 10 analytical requirements (AR-001 to AR-010), governed by 6 explicit business rules — source authority, GAAP/non-GAAP separation, period consistency, cash-flow integrity, CFPB proxy classification and GQI disclosure — plus defined acceptance criteria.

KPI governance

Every major KPI carries a documented definition, calculation logic, source and business purpose. Derived measures — products per member, segment revenue shares, OCF-to-net-income — are explicitly labelled as project-derived and reconciled to their sources.

Honest about limitations

No internal SoFi operational, customer-service or transaction data was available, and the analysis says so. CFPB complaints are treated strictly as an external proxy. Findings are presented as decision-support interpretation, not valuation advice.
